In detail

Why prompting is a core business skill

AI tools are only as useful as the instructions they receive. Prompting is the skill that turns AI from a novelty into a productive tool.

Good prompts save time at every stage. They reduce editing, cut down on repeated attempts and produce work that is closer to finished.

Prompting is also a transferable skill. The same principles apply across different AI tools and will continue to apply as tools improve.

When a whole team shares good prompts, quality becomes more consistent. Customers receive clearer, more professional communication.

Practical, task-based learning

This is a hands-on course. Attendees spend most of the day writing, testing and improving prompts.

For private sessions, we can use your own documents and examples. Sensitive information is replaced with sample data so nothing confidential is shared.

Each module ends with a short exercise based on real work. Attendees compare results and learn from each other.

The day finishes with each attendee building a personal set of ready-to-use prompts.

The five-part prompt framework

At the centre of the course is a simple framework for writing prompts. It covers five elements: role, task, context, format and tone.

The role tells the AI who to act as, such as a customer service manager or a technical writer. The task explains exactly what you need.

The context gives background, such as who the audience is and what they already know. The format sets out how the answer should look, such as a table, bullet points or a short email.

The tone describes the style, such as formal, friendly or concise.

Using all five elements consistently produces far better results than a single-line request.

Attendees practise the framework on several tasks until it becomes second nature.

Prompting for documents, data and research

Many of the biggest time savings come from working with existing information. The course covers how to prompt AI to work with documents and data.

Attendees learn to summarise long reports, extract key points from contracts and compare two versions of a document.

We also cover how to turn rough notes into structured reports, and how to convert meeting transcripts into clear action lists.

For spreadsheets, attendees learn to ask AI to explain formulas, spot patterns and suggest ways to present data.

We explain how to check AI summaries against the original source. This is essential for accuracy.

Throughout, we highlight which types of information should not be uploaded to AI tools.

Sharing prompts across your team

Good prompts are valuable business assets. When they are shared, the whole team benefits from the best results.

The course shows how to build a shared prompt library. It can be stored in a simple document, a shared drive or inside your AI tool.

We cover how to name, organise and update prompts so they stay useful over time.

Shared prompts also improve consistency. Customer emails, quotes and reports follow the same structure and tone, whoever writes them.

We recommend appointing a prompt owner in each team to keep the library up to date.

Attendees leave with a ready-made template to start their team's library straight away.

Common prompting mistakes and how to fix them

Most weak AI results come from a small number of common mistakes. The course shows how to recognise and fix each one.

The first mistake is being too vague. Asking AI to 'write an email' gives a generic result, while explaining the purpose, audience and key points produces something usable.

The second is giving no examples. Showing AI a good example of the output you want is one of the most effective techniques.

The third is accepting the first answer. Follow-up prompts such as 'make it shorter' or 'add a clear next step' quickly improve results.

The fourth is asking for too much at once. Breaking a large task into smaller steps produces better quality.

Attendees practise fixing each mistake with real examples.
