In detail

Why every business needs an AI policy

AI tools are easy to access, which means use can spread quickly without oversight. A clear policy prevents problems before they happen.

Without a policy, staff may enter customer details, pricing or confidential documents into public AI tools. This can breach data protection law and contracts.

A policy also sets standards for accuracy. AI can produce incorrect information that sounds convincing, so human review is essential.

A good policy is short, practical and easy to follow. It supports staff to use AI well, rather than simply banning it.

A practical, business-focused workshop

This workshop focuses on practical decisions, not theory. We guide your leadership team through each part of the policy.

We use your own business context throughout. This includes your sector, the data you handle and the tools your team already uses.

The session is delivered on site, which allows open discussion of sensitive topics.

You receive a written summary and a draft policy framework after the workshop, ready for review by your advisers.

What an AI policy should cover

An effective AI policy is clear and practical. It tells staff what they can do, not just what they cannot.

Most policies cover approved AI tools, permitted uses, prohibited uses and rules for handling data.

They also set expectations for checking accuracy, disclosing AI use where appropriate and protecting intellectual property.

A good policy names who is responsible for AI decisions and how staff can ask questions or raise concerns.

It should also explain how often the policy will be reviewed, as AI tools change quickly.

The workshop guides you through each of these areas and records your decisions.

Data protection and AI tools

Data protection is the most important area of any AI policy. UK GDPR applies whenever personal data is processed, including through AI tools.

Free consumer AI accounts may use the information entered to improve their services. Business accounts usually offer stronger privacy controls.

The workshop explains these differences and what to check in supplier terms.

We also cover customer and employee data, confidentiality obligations in contracts and the need for data protection impact assessments in some cases.

Clear rules protect your customers, your staff and your business reputation.

Your data protection lead or adviser should review the final rules before they are adopted.

Supporting staff to follow the policy

A policy only works if staff understand and follow it. The workshop covers how to communicate the policy clearly.

We recommend a short summary for all staff, with practical examples of what is allowed and what is not.

Training is also important. Staff who understand AI tools are more likely to use them safely.

We suggest a simple way for staff to request approval for new tools or uses. This encourages responsible innovation.

Regular reminders and updates help keep the policy front of mind as AI use grows.

You leave the workshop with a staff communication template ready to adapt.

Accuracy, transparency and customer trust

AI can produce errors that sound convincing. Your policy should set clear rules for checking any AI-assisted work before it is used.

This is especially important for customer-facing content, quotes, technical information and anything with legal or safety implications.

Transparency is also important. The workshop helps you decide when to tell customers that AI has been used, for example in chat tools or written responses.

We also discuss intellectual property. Businesses should understand who owns AI-generated content and the risks of using it in published material.

Clear rules in these areas protect customer trust and your reputation.

Your decisions are recorded in the draft policy framework.

FAQs

Common questions

Is this legal advice?

No. The workshop provides practical guidance and a draft framework. Your policy should be reviewed by your own legal or compliance advisers.

Who should attend?

Directors, senior managers and anyone responsible for HR, IT, data protection or compliance.

Can the workshop be delivered online?

Yes, although we recommend on site for open discussion. Online delivery is available on request.

How long does it take to finalise the policy?

Most businesses finalise their policy within a few weeks of the workshop, after review by their advisers.

Should staff be trained after the policy is agreed?

Yes. We recommend AI for business: the essentials for all staff, covering the new rules in practice.

We are a small business. Do we really need an AI policy?

Yes. Even a short, one-page policy reduces risk and gives staff clear guidance. Small businesses can be just as exposed to data and reputational risks.

How often should the policy be reviewed?

We recommend reviewing it at least every six months, or sooner if you introduce new AI tools or uses.
