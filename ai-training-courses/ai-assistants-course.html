Course overview

AI assistants that know your business.

General AI tools know a lot about the world, but nothing about your business. AI assistants change that.

An AI assistant is set up with your own instructions, documents and knowledge. It can answer questions, draft documents and support your team in a consistent way.

Examples include an assistant that answers staff questions about company policies, or one that drafts quotes using your price lists and templates.

This course teaches your team to design, build, test and roll out AI assistants. We cover custom GPTs in ChatGPT, Claude projects and Microsoft Copilot agents.

Each attendee builds a working assistant for a real business task during the course.

We build and use AI assistants across our own businesses. This course shares the methods that work in practice.

In detail

Practical uses for AI assistants

AI assistants work best for tasks that rely on consistent knowledge and repeat often.

Common uses include staff handbooks, onboarding support, sales proposal drafting, customer service replies and technical document searches.

Assistants can also support specialist teams. For example, an operations assistant can summarise job notes or check reports against a standard format.

During the course, we help each attendee choose a use case that will deliver clear value for their business.

Accuracy, security and governance

An AI assistant must give accurate, reliable answers. We teach structured testing methods to check quality before anyone relies on it.

Security is equally important. We cover which documents are suitable to share, how to control access and how to meet UK GDPR obligations.

We also explain the limits of AI assistants. Important decisions, such as legal, safety or financial matters, should always be reviewed by a qualified person.

Every attendee leaves with a governance checklist for managing assistants over time.

Writing instructions that produce consistent results

The instructions behind an AI assistant determine how it behaves. Clear, detailed instructions produce reliable answers.

The course teaches a structured approach to writing assistant instructions. It covers purpose, audience, tone, rules, format and what to do when the assistant does not know an answer.

We show how to set boundaries, so the assistant stays on topic and avoids giving advice outside its scope.

Attendees also learn to include example questions and ideal answers. These examples help the assistant match your expectations.

We review instructions together during the sessions and suggest improvements.

Strong instructions are the foundation of every successful assistant.

Preparing your documents for AI

An assistant is only as good as the information it uses. Poorly organised or outdated documents lead to weak answers.

The course shows how to prepare documents for AI. This includes removing outdated content, splitting long files into clear sections and using descriptive headings.

We explain which file types work best and how much information each platform can handle.

We also cover how to keep knowledge up to date. Assistants need a clear owner who refreshes documents when policies or prices change.

Sensitive information, such as personal data or confidential contracts, should only be added with appropriate controls in place.

Well-prepared documents greatly improve the accuracy of every answer.

Rolling out assistants to your team

A successful rollout needs more than a working assistant. Staff need to know what it does, when to use it and how to report problems.

The course covers how to introduce assistants with a short guide and demonstration.

We recommend starting with a small pilot group. Their feedback helps improve the assistant before wider rollout.

We also show how to track usage and gather feedback, so you can measure the value the assistant provides.

Clear ownership is essential. Each assistant should have a named person responsible for its accuracy and updates.

Attendees leave with a rollout plan for their own assistant.

Choosing the right platform

Several platforms now allow businesses to build their own AI assistants. The right choice depends on the tools you already use and your security needs.

Custom GPTs in ChatGPT are quick to build and easy to share. Claude projects handle long documents well and allow teams to work with shared knowledge.

Microsoft Copilot agents integrate with Microsoft 365, including SharePoint, Teams and Outlook. They suit businesses already using the Microsoft ecosystem.

Each platform has different pricing, data controls and sharing options.

During week one, we compare these platforms side by side and help each attendee choose the best fit.

The skills taught on the course apply to all of them.

FAQs

Common questions

Which platform should we build on?

It depends on the tools your business already uses. We compare the main options during week one and help you choose.

Is it safe to upload our documents?

It can be, with the right settings and business accounts. We cover data protection in detail and help you decide what is appropriate to share.

Will the assistant always be accurate?

No AI assistant is perfect. Careful instructions and testing greatly improve accuracy, and we teach how to keep people involved in important decisions.

Do we need paid AI subscriptions?

Building custom assistants usually requires a paid business plan. We explain the options and costs before the course starts.

Can you build an assistant for us?

Yes. Our team can design and build AI assistants for your business as a separate service.

How long does it take to build an assistant?

A simple assistant can be built in a few hours. Testing, improving and rolling it out properly usually takes a few weeks.

Can one assistant serve several teams?

It can, but focused assistants usually perform better. We often recommend separate assistants for distinct tasks or departments.
