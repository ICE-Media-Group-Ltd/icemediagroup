Course overview

Spend less time on admin and more on real work.

Every business has repetitive admin tasks. These include copying data between systems, sending routine emails, updating spreadsheets and preparing standard documents.

These tasks take hours each week and are prone to errors. AI and automation tools can now handle many of them reliably.

This course teaches your team to identify, plan and build simple automations. It combines AI assistants with no-code automation tools such as Zapier and Microsoft Power Automate.

No programming knowledge is required. Every automation is built step by step during the live sessions.

The four-week format allows time to practise between sessions. Each attendee builds at least one working automation for their own role.

In detail

What can be automated with AI?

Many everyday tasks follow the same steps each time. These are ideal candidates for automation.

Examples include logging new enquiries in a spreadsheet, sending confirmation emails, creating job summaries and preparing weekly reports.

AI adds a new level of capability. It can read emails, understand documents and write responses, which older automation tools could not do.

The course helps each attendee find the tasks in their own role that will save the most time.

Keeping automations safe and reliable

Automation must be reliable and secure. A poorly built automation can send incorrect information or expose sensitive data.

We teach attendees to include human approval steps for important actions. For example, AI can draft a reply, but a person approves it before it is sent.

We also cover data protection, access controls and how to test automations before they go live.

Every attendee leaves with a simple checklist for building safe automations.

Introducing no-code automation tools

No-code automation tools connect different apps so they can share information automatically. They work through simple visual builders rather than code.

Zapier connects thousands of business apps and is easy to learn. Microsoft Power Automate is built into Microsoft 365 and suits businesses that already use Outlook, Teams and SharePoint.

Both tools follow a simple pattern. A trigger starts the automation, such as a new email arriving, and one or more actions follow, such as adding a row to a spreadsheet.

AI steps can be added in the middle. For example, AI can read an email, summarise it and decide which team should handle it.

The course explains how to choose the right tool for your business.

Attendees build their first automation during week one.

Example automations for service businesses

The course uses examples that are directly relevant to service businesses.

One example logs every new website enquiry into a spreadsheet, sends a confirmation email and notifies the right team member.

Another reads incoming supplier invoices, extracts key details and adds them to a tracking sheet for review.

A third prepares a weekly summary of completed jobs from your job management system and sends it to managers.

Other examples include meeting note summaries, appointment reminders and follow-up emails after quotes.

Attendees adapt these examples to their own processes during the course.

Measuring time saved

Automation should deliver clear, measurable benefits. The course shows how to calculate the time and cost each automation saves.

Attendees record how long a task takes manually and how often it happens. This gives a simple estimate of hours saved each month.

We also consider other benefits, such as fewer errors, faster response times and better customer experience.

These figures help managers decide which automations to prioritise and whether paid tool plans are worth the cost.

We recommend reviewing automations every few months to confirm they still work and still save time.

By the end of the course, each attendee can present a clear business case for their automation.

Combining AI with your existing systems

Most businesses already use several systems, such as email, spreadsheets, accounting software and job management tools. Automation connects these systems so information flows between them.

The course shows how to check whether your systems can be connected. Many popular business tools have ready-made connections to Zapier and Power Automate.

Where direct connections are not available, we explain alternatives such as email parsing, shared spreadsheets and webhooks.

We also cover how to work with your IT provider or system suppliers when access permissions are needed.

This practical approach means automations fit around your current setup, without replacing systems you rely on.

Attendees map their own systems during week one.

FAQs

Common questions

Do attendees need coding skills?

No. The course uses no-code tools. If you can use email and spreadsheets confidently, you can build automations.

Which systems can be connected?

Most common business tools can be connected, including Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, accounting software and many CRM and job management systems.

Will we need to pay for automation tools?

Most tools have free plans suitable for learning. We will explain the costs of paid plans if your business needs them.

How much time is needed between sessions?

We recommend one to two hours a week to practise and complete tasks between sessions.

Can you build the automations for us?

Yes. If you prefer, our team can plan and build automations for your business as a separate service.

Is automation suitable for small businesses?

Yes. Small businesses often benefit the most, as a few hours saved each week can make a big difference to a small team.

What happens if an automation stops working?

We teach attendees how to set up error alerts and check automation logs, so problems are spotted and fixed quickly.
